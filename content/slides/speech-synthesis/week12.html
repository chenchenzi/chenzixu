Prosody · above the phones

Prosody: pitch, duration and loudness

PITCH · F0

The tune

How the voice rises and falls over a phrase: a final rise, a final fall, a peak on one word

DURATION

The timing

How long each syllable lasts, and where the pauses fall: lengthening marks stress and phrase ends

ENERGY

The loudness

Stressed and accented syllables are louder; function words are quieter

  • Prosody can also include voice quality (e.g. creaky, breathy voice), rhythm, and more
  • What it signals: statement or question, which word is in focus, where a phrase ends, the speaker's attitude
  • The text gives the words; it rarely spells out the tune
★ The hardest idea this week

One sentence, four tunes, four meanings

Only the pitch changes; the words never do.
Where pitch carries more of the meaning

Singapore English particles carry meaning in pitch

  • English: pitch works over the phrase (intonation); the words stay the same words
  • Mandarin: pitch tells words apart (lexical tone): mā, má, mǎ, mà are four different words
  • Singapore English particles (lah, lor, meh) end the phrase and show the speaker's attitude; some came from Cantonese with their tones (Lim 2007)
  • meh, a skeptical “really?”, is described with a high level pitch (Soh, Lee & Tan 2022); none of the three TTS voices ends it high
MEASURED · THREE TTS VOICES

End of “The shop is open …”, in semitones from each sentence's median

Teal: the sentence ends below its median. Synthetic voices, made in advance; the end is the last 100 ms of voicing.

Why TTS voices sound flat

Averaging many tunes gives a flatter tune

  • The same words were read with different tunes in the training data
  • A model trained to be close on average learns the average tune: smaller rises and falls than a typical real reading
  • Getting the tune back: predict pitch and let people edit it (FastSpeech 2); copy the tune of a recording; or mark it in the text with tags (SSML)
How to measure a tune

Measure the tune in semitones, not hertz

semitones = 12 × log2( f2 / f1 )

f1 = the starting pitch, f2 = the ending pitch, both in Hz

  • Pitch is heard by ratio: 100 → 120 Hz (+20 Hz) and 200 → 240 Hz (+40 Hz) sound like the same step
  • 12 semitones = one octave = double the frequency
  • To ask “does the sentence rise?”, compare the last 100 ms of voicing with the sentence's median pitch (its middle value): positive = ends higher, negative = ends lower
WORKED OUT

Coral: a rise; teal: a fall. Each value computed from the formula. In the practical, this formula is the one line of code you write.

Part II · whose voice is it?

The tune says how it is meant.
The voice says who is saying it.

Zero-shot voice cloning

A few seconds of audio is enough to clone a voice

A few seconds of someone's voicea phone call, a video, a voice note

→
TTS modelany text you type

Any sentence, in that voicewords the person never said
  • VALL-E (Microsoft, 2023): 3 seconds; demos only, never released
  • Qwen3-TTS (Alibaba, Jan 2026): “3-second” voice cloning; open weights, free to download
  • Pocket TTS (Kyutai, Jan 2026): about 5 seconds, on a laptop, no GPU
  • VibeVoice (Microsoft, Aug 2025): released openly, then its voice code withdrawn after misuse
A VOICE CAN BE PERSONAL DATA

Nothing in the model checks whose voice it is: any check has to be added by people.

In Singapore, a recording can be personal data under the PDPA if a person can be identified from it (PDPC advisory guidelines, revised 2024).

What can stop misuse

Four safeguards, and none is enough alone

1 · before cloning

Consent

Ask the speaker first, in writing

Apple Personal Voice builds only your own voice; the US actors' union (SAG-AFTRA) requires consent and pay

Gap: only honest users ask

2 · when audio is made

Marking

Hide a watermark in the audio, or attach a signed label of where it came from

AudioSeal (2024); C2PA labels

Gap: labels can be stripped; watermarks attacked

3 · when audio arrives

Detection

A classifier checks whether the speech is synthetic

the ASVspoof challenge, since 2015

Gap: much worse on generators it has not seen

4 · after harm

Law

Penalties and takedowns, which also deter

US FCC (2024): AI voices in robocalls need consent; Singapore (2024 Act): no faked candidates online during an election; EU AI Act: marking from Aug 2026

Gap: slow, and stops at borders

Each closes one gap. Together they raise the cost of misuse; none makes it impossible.

The same technology, the other direction

Cloning also gives people their own voice back

  • Voice banking (VocaliD, this week's TED reading): people with ALS (motor neuron disease) or facing removal of the larynx record phrases while they can still speak
  • Apple Personal Voice (2023): 150 phrases, about 15 minutes, built on the phone; 10 phrases since 2025
  • July 2024: US Representative Jennifer Wexton, who lost much of her speech to a brain disease (progressive supranuclear palsy), spoke in Congress in an AI clone of her earlier voice
ONE TREND, TWO USES

Voice banking once needed 350 to 1,600 recorded phrases; now 10 can be enough.

The less audio a voice needs, the easier it is for patients, and for scammers.

Course definitions, with sources

This week, formally

DEFINITION
Prosody

The pitch, duration and loudness patterns of speech above the level of single phones, across syllables, words and phrases; in J&M's words, “the use of F0, energy, and duration to convey pragmatic, affective, or conversation-interactional meanings”.

Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed. draft), ch. 15 §15.3 (Aug 2026 draft; §14.3 in the Jan 2026 release).
DEFINITION
Intonation (tune)

The rise and fall of F0 over a phrase, used to signal meanings such as statement versus question, focus and attitude; a final rise on a yes-no question is called a question rise.

Course wording. J&M ch. 15 §15.3.3 “Tune” (Aug 2026 draft).
DEFINITION
Voice cloning

Making a speech synthesizer speak in a particular person's voice from recordings of that person; zero-shot cloning does it from a few seconds of audio, without retraining the model.

Course wording. Jia et al. (2018); Wang et al. (2023), VALL-E.
DEFINITION
Informed consent (for a voice)

Agreement, given freely and in advance, by a person who understands what their voice will be used for, by whom, for how long, and how to withdraw.

Course wording. NAVA (2026), voice actors' AI contract terms; SAG-AFTRA (2023); EU GDPR Art. 7 (withdrawal).

Before you go

Four things to remember

1. Prosody (pitch, duration, loudness) carries meaning the words do not: question or statement, focus, phrasing, attitude; Singapore English particles carry theirs partly in pitch

2. TTS voices often get the words right and the tune wrong: one text allows many tunes, and a model trained on averages flattens them

3. Measure a tune in semitones: 12 × log2(f2/f1), the end of the sentence against its median

4. A few seconds of audio can clone a voice; consent, watermarks, detection and law each help, and none is enough alone

Appendix: worked answers

Answers

EX 12.1

12 × log2(270 / 180) = 12 × log2(1.5) ≈ +7.0 semitones. Yes: a large rise, more than half an octave.

EX 12.2

It has missed the intonation: in “You're leaving?” only a final rise makes it a question. The one number: the question's end minus the statement's end (each the last 100 ms against the sentence's median), in semitones; it should be clearly positive.

EX 12.3

The same text appears with different tunes in training; the tune that is closest to all of them on average is their average, which rises and falls less than a typical reading. To get it back: predict pitch explicitly and edit it (FastSpeech 2), copy the style of a reference recording, or mark it in the text with SSML.

EX 12.4

For example: scam calls in a relative's voice (safeguard: call back on a number you know; consent checks in the product) and fake statements by a politician (safeguard: watermarks and signed records of where a file came from; election laws against deepfakes). Bank voice logins (safeguard: a second factor) also work.
