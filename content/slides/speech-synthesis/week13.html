Quiz 2, B1(a)(i) · one cell at a time

Each cell keeps the best route into it

V(s, t) = P(xt | s) × max over routes of [ V(prev, t−1) × P(prev → s) ]

V(s, t): the probability of the best path that ends in state s at frame t

a route: one cell one frame back, times the transition out of it

max: keep the biggest route, remember where it came from, discard the rest

Colors, in every figure today

Quiz 2, B1(a)(i) · the last column

Compare the routes, not the cells

Two routes arrive at /i/ in frame 3
  • 0.18 is the larger cell, and it loses: its transition is the smaller one
  • A route is a cell times the transition out of it, so both parts count
  • Keep 0.06, and remember that it came from /s/
WHAT THIS COSTS

Comparing the cells instead of the routes gives 0.0216 and the path /s/ → /i/ → /i/. It was the most common wrong answer, and it scored nothing on (a)(ii).

Quiz 2, B1(a)(ii) · reading the answer off

Follow the kept routes, not the maxima

  • Start at the cell the word has to end in, then follow the route each cell kept
  • A cell holds the best path into it, and those paths need not agree with each other
WHY 0.008 AND NOT 0

V(/s/, 3) scores the best route into that cell, and a cell knows nothing about what follows it. The end-in-/i/ rule applies when you read the answer off, not while you fill the table: with a fourth frame, /s/ is live again.

A question from the room

A word model only runs forward

The word model

An arc from /i/ back to /s/ would mean the pronunciation went /s i s/, which is a different word. The rows also leave no room for it: 0.4 + 0.6 and 0.3 + 0.7 already come to 1.

The ice-cream model

Hot and cold can follow each other in any order, so every arc exists and the matrix is full. Nothing fixes the sequence in advance.

What is still shared

The same recursion, the same table, the same backtrace. Only the set of allowed arcs differs, and that is what the word's spelling buys you.

Quiz 2, B1 · what the table buys you

One table does the work of every path

WHY THE REUSE IS SAFE

Nothing is approximated: what comes next depends only on the state a path is in, not on the way in, so a dropped route could never have won later.
